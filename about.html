The Workshop

A container for the poems you've been avoiding.

The Invitation

A writers workshop series exploring the cyclical nature of healing — led by poet Leroy D. Bean. Across six Sundays, you'll write into two acts. The first is the leap, the shedding, the version of you that doesn't ask permission. The second is what's underneath when the music stops, the thing you keep circling back to no matter how far you run.

Two guest poets — Coco and Naturally Cherry — join for the final three sessions to help you braid your voice with another's. They've already walked this road. They know the production. They'll meet you where you are and push you where you need to go.

What You Leave With

More than a poem.

A finished piece.

Every poet leaves this workshop with a piece they wrote here — one that's been shaped, challenged, and spoken aloud. Something you couldn't have written alone.

Bean's mentorship.

Six weeks under the guidance of Leroy D. Bean — a spoken word poet with twelve years on stages from theaters and universities to festivals and poetry slams, and a workshop facilitator who builds emotional intelligence through the craft of storytelling. Bean doesn't just teach — he holds the room, names what's unfinished, and stays with you through the work. For finalists, that mentorship continues all the way through rehearsals and the stage.

A private call with Bean.

Every participant receives one private mentor call with Bean, scheduled anytime between January 10 and February 21. If you know you'll miss a session, let Bean know ahead of time — your call can be used to cover what you missed, and you stay eligible to submit as a finalist. Emergencies are handled at Bean's discretion. Finalists receive additional mentorship leading up to the show date.

Mentorship from anchored poets.

Coco and Naturally Cherry are performing poets in the 2027 production. They join for Sessions 4–6 as mentors — poets who've already lived inside this material and can guide you through the braiding of voices, the layering of selves, the final shaping of your piece.

A room of poets who showed up.

This is a cohort, not an audience. You'll write alongside other poets who chose to be here — people willing to read the hard thing out loud and sit with what comes after. The connections made in this room tend to outlast the sessions themselves.

A path to the stage.

Two finalists are chosen to perform their piece live in Dayton in front of up to 200 people, September–October 2027. The selection is based on the work you submit. The stage is real, the audience is real, and Bean walks with you the entire way.
